"Write two pages" is a common instruction, but pages are measured in words only indirectly. Here is how to translate between pages and word counts, and what lengths are typical for everyday writing.

## The rule of thumb

For a standard document with a 12-point font such as Times New Roman, one-inch margins and no large images:

- **Single-spaced:** about 500 words per page.
- **Double-spaced:** about 250 to 275 words per page.

These are rough averages. Real pages differ depending on several factors.

## What changes the count

- **Font and size.** A wider font or a larger size fits fewer words on a page.
- **Margins.** Wider margins reduce the text area.
- **Line spacing.** Double spacing roughly halves the number of words compared with single spacing.
- **Headings, lists and images.** White space around them uses room that text would otherwise fill.
- **Paragraph length.** Many short paragraphs leave more blank space than a few long ones.

If an assignment specifies a page count, the safest approach is to match the formatting rules you were given and check the page count in your word processor.

## Typical lengths for common writing

These ranges are common guidelines rather than strict rules, and the right length always depends on your reader and purpose:

- **A short email:** often between 50 and 200 words.
- **A five-paragraph school essay:** around 500 to 800 words.
- **A blog post:** many readers expect 800 to 2,000 words, depending on topic depth.
- **A news article:** frequently 400 to 800 words.
- **A cover letter:** usually under one page, so around 250 to 400 words.
- **A product description:** often 50 to 300 words.

## How to count words accurately

Different programs count slightly differently. Hyphenated words, numbers, abbreviations and symbols can be treated as one word or several. A few practical tips:

1. Decide whether titles, headings, captions, footnotes and the bibliography count toward the limit. Many assignments exclude references.
2. Count with the same tool throughout, so your totals stay consistent.
3. Leave a small margin. If the limit is 1,000 words, aim for roughly 950 to 1,000.

## Writing to a target

When you need to reach a word count, add substance instead of padding. Add an example, a definition or a counterargument. When you need to cut, remove repeated points, long introductions and filler phrases such as "it is important to note that".
