Large images slow down websites, fill up storage and bounce back from email attachments. The good news is that you can often cut file size dramatically with no visible difference. Follow these steps in order.

## 1. Resize to the size you actually need

Modern phone cameras produce photos that are thousands of pixels wide. If an image appears in an 800-pixel column on a web page, anything beyond roughly twice that width (for sharp high-density screens) is wasted. Resizing is usually the single biggest saving, because file size grows quickly with pixel count.

## 2. Choose the right format

- Use JPG or WebP for photographs.
- Use PNG or lossless WebP for logos, icons and screenshots with text.
- Avoid saving a photograph as PNG unless you need lossless quality, since the file will be much larger.

## 3. Lower the quality setting a little

For JPG and lossy WebP, a quality setting between 70 and 85 is often a good balance for photos. Differences in that range are hard to spot at normal viewing size, while the file can shrink considerably. Compare the result with the original at 100 percent zoom before you commit.

## 4. Remove metadata you do not need

Photos can carry hidden data such as camera model, date and sometimes GPS location. Removing it saves a few kilobytes and improves privacy when you share a picture publicly. Many editing tools offer a "strip metadata" or "export without location" option.

## 5. Avoid compressing the same file again and again

Every time you edit and re-save a JPG, quality drops slightly. Keep an original copy, make edits on that, and export the compressed version once.

## 6. Do not compress what is already small

Tiny icons and simple graphics gain little from further compression. Focus your effort on the largest files first.

## A realistic example

Say a photo straight from a phone is 4,000 pixels wide and several megabytes in size. Resizing it to 1,600 pixels and exporting it at quality 80 as WebP or JPG will often bring it well under a few hundred kilobytes, and on a normal screen it looks almost identical. Results vary with the picture, so treat these numbers as an illustration rather than a promise.

## Tips for websites

- Provide images at the displayed size instead of letting the browser scale them down.
- Load images below the fold only when the visitor scrolls near them (lazy loading).
- Add descriptive alt text for accessibility and search.
