Choosing the wrong image format can make a page slow, a logo blurry or a photo unnecessarily large. Here is how the three most common web formats differ and when to use each.

## JPG (JPEG)

JPG uses lossy compression: it discards detail the eye barely notices to make files much smaller.

- **Best for:** photographs and images with smooth color gradients.
- **Strengths:** small files and universal support.
- **Limits:** no transparency, and sharp edges and text can look fuzzy. Each time you re-save a JPG, a little more quality is lost.

## PNG

PNG uses lossless compression, so the image is stored without losing any detail.

- **Best for:** logos, icons, screenshots, diagrams and anything with text or hard edges.
- **Strengths:** crisp results and support for transparent backgrounds.
- **Limits:** photographs in PNG are often several times larger than the same photo as a JPG.

## WebP

WebP is a modern format that can work in lossy or lossless mode, and it supports transparency and animation.

- **Best for:** images on websites, where smaller files mean faster pages.
- **Strengths:** files are often noticeably smaller than JPG or PNG at similar visual quality.
- **Limits:** current major browsers support it, but some older programs, email clients and print services may not accept it.

## Quick decision guide

- **Photo for a website:** WebP, with JPG as the safe alternative.
- **Photo to send to someone or upload to a form:** JPG, because it opens everywhere.
- **Logo or icon with a transparent background:** PNG or WebP.
- **Screenshot with text:** PNG, or lossless WebP.
- **Animated graphic:** WebP or a short video, rather than a very large GIF.

## Quality settings

For lossy formats, the quality slider trades size for detail. Values around 75 to 85 are a common starting point for photos. Lower values shrink files further but can create blocky areas around edges. Always check the result at full size before you decide.

## Resolution matters too

Format is only part of the story. A 4,000-pixel-wide photo shown in an 800-pixel space wastes bandwidth no matter which format you use. Resize the image to the largest size it will actually be displayed at.
