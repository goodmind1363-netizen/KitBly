Slow or unreliable Wi-Fi is frustrating, but the cause is often easy to fix. Start by finding out where the problem is, then work through these steps.

## First, find the source of the problem

1. Test your speed with a device connected to the router by a cable. This shows the speed your internet plan actually delivers.
2. Test again over Wi-Fi, standing near the router.
3. Test in the room where the connection feels slow.

If the cable result is far below your plan, the issue is probably with your provider or router. If the cable test is fine but Wi-Fi is slow, the problem is your wireless setup, and these tips will help.

## 1. Restart the router

Unplug it for about 30 seconds, then plug it back in. This clears temporary glitches and is worth trying first.

## 2. Move the router

Place it in a central, elevated and open spot. Avoid cupboards, floors, thick walls, mirrors and large metal objects. Keep it away from microwaves and cordless phone bases, which can cause interference.

## 3. Use the 5 GHz band where you can

Most routers offer two bands. The 2.4 GHz band reaches farther but is slower and more crowded. The 5 GHz band is faster over shorter distances. Connect nearby devices to 5 GHz and keep 2.4 GHz for far-away devices.

## 4. Change the Wi-Fi channel

In apartment buildings, neighboring networks compete on the same channels. Most routers can choose a channel automatically. If yours does not, try a different one in the settings.

## 5. Update the router firmware

Manufacturers release updates that fix bugs and improve stability. Check your router's admin page, or its companion app, for an update option.

## 6. Reduce the load

Many devices, cloud backups and game downloads running at once can saturate the connection. Pause large downloads while you work or make calls.

## 7. Cover large homes properly

If one router cannot reach every room, a mesh system or an additional access point usually works better than a basic range extender. Where possible, connect extra units to the router with a cable.

## 8. Wire what you can

Fixed devices such as a desktop, a TV or a game console run best on Ethernet. This frees up wireless capacity for phones and laptops.

## When to contact your provider

If the cable test stays well below the speed you pay for, even after restarting the router, contact your provider and share your test results.
