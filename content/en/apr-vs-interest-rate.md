When you compare loans, two percentages appear in every offer: the interest rate and the APR. They are related but not the same, and mixing them up can make an expensive loan look cheap.

## The interest rate

The interest rate is the price of borrowing the money itself, expressed as a yearly percentage of the amount you owe. It determines how much interest builds up each month and, together with the term, your monthly payment.

## The APR

APR stands for annual percentage rate. It expresses the yearly cost of the loan including certain mandatory costs, such as arrangement fees, on top of the interest. Many countries require lenders to show it, sometimes under a local name. In France you may see TAEG, and in Germany the effective annual interest rate (effektiver Jahreszins). Exact rules differ by country, so treat the label on your own offer as the reference.

Because it bundles fees with interest, the APR is usually the better number for comparing offers.

## An example where the lower rate costs more

Imagine you need 10,000 for 3 years and receive two offers:

- **Offer A:** 5.0% interest and a 300 arrangement fee deducted from the amount you receive.
- **Offer B:** 5.3% interest and no fee.

At first glance Offer A looks cheaper. Here is what the numbers say:

- Offer A: monthly payment about 299.71, total repaid about 10,789.52. Because you only receive 9,700, the effective yearly cost is about 7.3%.
- Offer B: monthly payment about 301.06, total repaid about 10,838.08, with an effective yearly cost of about 5.4%.

Offer B costs a little more each month but is clearly cheaper overall once the fee is counted. These figures are illustrative calculations. Your lender's official APR may be computed with country-specific rules.

## What APR may not include

- Optional insurance you choose to add.
- Late payment fees or early repayment charges.
- Future changes, if the loan has a variable rate.

## How to compare offers in practice

1. Compare offers with the same amount and the same term. APR is not meaningful across very different terms.
2. Ask for the total amount you will repay and for a written breakdown of every fee.
3. Read the standard information sheet your lender should give you before you sign.
4. Use a loan calculator to see the monthly payment for each interest rate, then compare it with the APR.

## The short version

The interest rate tells you what the money costs. The APR tells you what the whole loan costs. When two offers look close, the APR and the total repaid are the numbers that settle it.
