Internet plans are sold by speed, but it is hard to know what number you actually need. The answer depends on what you do online and how many people share the connection.

## Mbps and MB/s are different

Internet speed is measured in megabits per second (Mbps). File sizes are usually shown in megabytes (MB), and one byte is eight bits. So a 100 Mbps connection can transfer at most about 12.5 megabytes per second under ideal conditions. Real-world results are usually lower.

## Typical needs by activity

These figures are rough guidelines, and individual services publish their own recommendations that can change over time:

- **Email, messaging and web browsing:** a few Mbps is enough.
- **Video streaming in HD:** around 5 Mbps per stream.
- **Video streaming in 4K:** often 15 to 25 Mbps per stream.
- **Video calls:** a few Mbps in each direction for HD quality.
- **Online gaming:** low bandwidth, but it needs a stable connection with low latency.
- **Large downloads, backups and game updates:** the faster the better, because time saved grows with speed.

## How to size your plan

Add up what could happen at the same moment. Example for a household of four:

- Two HD streams: about 10 Mbps.
- One video call for work: about 3 to 5 Mbps.
- Browsing and phones: about 5 Mbps.

The total is around 20 Mbps. To leave room for peaks and for devices that update in the background, many households choose a plan two to three times higher, so 50 Mbps or more would be comfortable here. Larger households, several 4K streams or heavy remote work call for more.

## Do not forget upload speed

Video calls, cloud backups and sending large files rely on upload speed. Many home plans offer much lower upload than download. If you work from home, check the upload figure as well.

## Advertised speed versus real speed

Providers usually advertise a maximum "up to" speed. What you get depends on:

- Whether you use Wi-Fi or a cable connection.
- Distance from the router and walls in between.
- How many devices are active.
- Congestion on your provider's network at busy times.

## How to measure it properly

1. Close other apps and pause downloads.
2. If possible, connect a computer to the router with a cable for a baseline result.
3. Test again over Wi-Fi in the room where you actually work.
4. Repeat at different times of day and compare.
