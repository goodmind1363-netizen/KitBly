You attach a PDF to an email and it bounces, or a form refuses your upload because the file is too large. Here is why PDFs get big and how to bring them down to a manageable size.

## Why PDFs get large

A PDF made from typed text is usually small. The size explodes when it contains:

- **Scanned pages.** Each page is a photo, often at very high resolution and in color.
- **High-resolution images** pasted from cameras or design files.
- **Many embedded fonts** or complex vector graphics.

## How big is too big?

Limits depend on the service. Many email providers cap attachments at roughly 20 to 25 MB, and a lot of online forms set far lower limits, sometimes just a few megabytes. Check the exact limit before you start so you know your target.

## Ways to make a PDF smaller

1. **Export again with a smaller setting.** In many word processors, choosing a "minimum size" option when saving as PDF can shrink the file.
2. **Scan more sensibly.** For ordinary documents, a resolution of about 150 to 200 dpi is usually readable, and grayscale or black-and-white scans are far smaller than color.
3. **Reduce image resolution.** Images meant for the screen rarely need more than about 150 dpi.
4. **Remove pages you do not need.** Delete blank pages and unnecessary attachments.
5. **Split the document.** If a file is still too large, send it in two parts.

## How browser-based compression works

Our PDF compressor takes a practical approach: it draws every page, saves each one as a compressed image and rebuilds the PDF from those images. You choose how strong the compression is:

- **Light:** higher quality, a larger file.
- **Medium:** a balanced default.
- **Strong:** the smallest file, with visibly lower quality on fine detail.

Because pages are saved as images, **the text can no longer be selected or searched** after compression. That trade-off works well for scanned documents and image-heavy files, but it is a poor choice for a text document you want people to copy from. If the result is not smaller than your original, keep the original.

## Privacy

The file is processed on your device inside your browser. It is not uploaded to a server, which matters for contracts, IDs and other sensitive documents.

## Quick checklist before you send

- Open the compressed PDF and check that every page is readable.
- Keep the original file safe in case you need the full quality version.
- Name the file clearly so the recipient knows what it is.
