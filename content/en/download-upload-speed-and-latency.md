A speed test shows several numbers, and the biggest one is not always the one that matters. Here is what each result means and when it affects you.

## Download speed

Download speed is how fast data arrives from the internet to your device. It matters for streaming video, loading web pages, downloading files and installing updates. Most plans advertise this number.

## Upload speed

Upload speed is how fast your device sends data out. It matters for:

- Video calls, where your camera and microphone stream to others.
- Backing up photos or files to the cloud.
- Sending large attachments.
- Live streaming and online gaming uploads.

Many home plans have much lower upload than download. That is normal, but it can become the bottleneck if you work from home.

## Latency (ping)

Latency is the time, in milliseconds (ms), it takes for a small piece of data to travel to a server and back. Lower is better. As a rough guide:

- **Under about 30 ms:** excellent.
- **30 to 60 ms:** good for nearly everything.
- **Above about 100 ms:** you may notice lag in fast online games and on calls.

Latency depends on distance, the type of connection and how busy the network is. Satellite and mobile connections often have higher latency than fiber or cable.

## Jitter

Jitter is how much latency varies from one moment to the next. A connection with low average latency but high jitter can still cause choppy calls and stuttering games, because the data does not arrive at a steady pace.

## Packet loss

Data travels in small packets. Packet loss means some do not arrive and have to be sent again. Even a small percentage can cause freezing video, robotic audio and slow pages. A healthy connection shows little or none.

## Which number should you care about?

- **Streaming and browsing:** download speed.
- **Video calls and cloud backups:** upload speed, latency and stability.
- **Gaming:** latency, jitter and packet loss matter more than raw speed.

## Why results change between tests

Wi-Fi signal strength, other devices using the network, the time of day and the server you test against all affect the results. Run the test a few times and look at the typical value, not the best or the worst.
